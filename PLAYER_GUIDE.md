# Warband Reimagined - Player guide

*A route through the mod from the first day to a Legacy: what to do first, where the big stories begin and what each one needs. Back to the [README](README.md), the [feature list](FEATURES.md), the [changelog](CHANGELOG.md) or the [testing guide](TESTING.md).*

**Nothing on this page is required.** The mod never forces a path: you can ride, trade, fight or rule as you please. Use this page when you want to know what is out there and in what order it opens up.

**Written from the mod's rules.** The days, renown values and prices below are the ones the game uses in version 11.8. If something differs in your game, please report it.

## The short version

1. Create a character. For a first game, keep the recommended rules (section 1).
2. In your first town, open **Walk the quarters of the town** and **News, notices and events**. Take work from the people and from the notice board.
3. Spend the first month building a company of about a dozen and reaching renown 80 to 100.
4. Start your first great story (section 4) and join one organisation (section 5).
5. Do your companions' stories as your battles together add up (section 6).
6. Choose your place in the world: a king's vassal, a soldier, a merchant, an outlaw, or the lord of your own land (section 7).
7. Later the world crises come, one at a time, each with its own story (section 8).
8. Reach one of the 42 Legacies (section 9). Then ride on, or close the chronicle.

## 1. Before you start

### Who you were

Character creation asks who your father was, how you grew up, what you did as an adult and why you left. Then you choose what you became. That choice sets how the game opens and gives you something every week.

| Origin | How you begin | What it gives you every week |
|---|---|---|
| Free adventurer | At a town's tournament festival | Nothing owed, so every week teaches you something |
| Mercenary captain | With an offer of paid service | A company of your size earns a name |
| Merchant heir | With the family warehouse | The family house sends its share |
| Hunted outlaw | Wanted in the town's realm | Old friends on the Grey Road send your cut |
| Pilgrim | At the nearest monastery | The road of prayer keeps you humble |
| Disgraced noble | With a lord who knew the family | Your name still carries in halls |
| Veteran soldier | Outside the camp of the host you left | Old drills stay sharp |

Why you left counts too: revenge brings notoriety, loss brings piety, wanderlust brings the trust of common folk, being forced out brings honour, and greed brings a heavier purse.

For a first game, the free adventurer and the mercenary captain are the simplest starts. One opens at a festival, the other with paid work.

### The rules of your chronicle

The last page of character creation sets the rules of the world. The defaults are the recommended rules, and **Restore the recommended rules** puts them back. Difficulty can be changed later in Options.

| Rule | Choices | Default |
|---|---|---|
| World crises begin | Day 230, 400, 600 or never | Day 230 |
| The Black Khergit Horde rises | Day 200, day 350 or never | Day 200 |
| Total war | Every kingdom starts at war, or not | Off |
| How you begin | With nothing but your sword, with a village, as a sworn vassal with a village, or as the ruler of one town | With nothing but your sword |
| Life on the roads | Quiet, lively or crowded | Lively |
| Hostile wanderers | On or off | On (the early game stays gentle, see section 3) |
| Permadeath | On or off (your heir carries on) | Off |
| Difficulty | Story, Gentle, Standard, Hard, Brutal | Standard |

You can also choose how many bandits roam and whether to play the short prologue at Zendar.

For a slower first game, move the crises to day 400 and the Horde to day 350. That leaves more time to find your feet before the world turns on you.

## 2. Your first day

You ride into your first town through its gate. A welcome box then names the three places that hold everything:

- **Reports** (on the map bar): your character sheet with every standing value and what it brings, the Chronicle of Deeds, statistics, the roads, the realms and your budget.
- **Notes**: every story in progress is under Quests. The in-game Guide is under Game Concepts.
- **Camp > The Chronicle**: where you act. Your house and heir, your company, the royal court, careers, your person and the options.

### The two town options that matter most

- **News, notices and events.** The notice board (jobs that pay on completion), the season's festival, and later "The troubles of the age".
- **Walk the quarters of the town.** The Market Quarter, the Temple Quarter, Guild Row, the Harbour (coastal towns), the Garrison Yard and the Low Town. Each quarter lists the people who work there.

Inside the quarters:

- **Ask around: who is in town?** lists who is here today.
- **Look for somebody who is waiting to be asked** takes you to the people who have a story for you.
- In each quarter you can walk in and talk to people at their posts (press Tab to leave), or choose **Speak with someone without walking about (quick)**.

Villages have a Village life page, and castles a Castle affairs page, that work the same way.

### Work and favours

Ask any of the townsfolk or villagers "Have you any work for me?" and they give you a job in their own trade. Once someone knows you, they may tell you a problem of their own: a personal story that changes what they can do for you afterwards. After three jobs for the same person, say "I have done well by you" and they offer one lasting favour.

### How to read a story page

- An option you cannot take yet is greyed, with the reason in brackets: a skill, a rank, an item, a friend or a trait.
- There is always one option that needs no skill and no gold, though it may cost you something else.
- Every option that costs money names its price. On the road, every attack line names what it will cost you in relations, honour and Wanted level.
- Some steps have a time limit, and the story fails if you let it run out.
- When you win a scene (hold a gate, catch a runner, keep a night watch), the next page says so under its text: "Done so far: ...".

## 3. Days 1 to 30: a small company

**Aim for:** about a dozen men, a few thousand denars, and renown 80 to 100.

### Earning your first money

- The notice board in every town, under News, notices and events.
- Jobs from the people of the quarters and villages.
- Bounties from the Bounty Clerk, courier runs, and the sheriff's jobs.
- Trading with Guild convoys and horse traders on the road, who buy and sell at their own prices.
- Camp > Camp activities: hunt, forage, drill, dice, spar, or scout for hideouts. Each takes a few hours and can be done once a day.

### The roads are gentle at first

Every party on the map is met by talk. You can trade with it, escort it, duel its champion, hire from it, ride with it or rob it.

Hostile bands start far away, and they leave a company of fewer than twelve alone unless you provoke them. Staying under twelve is a safe way to learn the map. Grow past it when you are ready to fight.

### What comes to you in the first weeks

| When | What happens |
|---|---|
| Day 7 | **Blood and Ashes** begins by itself and sends you to the Ruins of Old Ruvar |
| From day 15, at renown 80 | The scholar Idris stops your column with **The Emperor's Regalia**. If you send him away, you can reconsider from the camp menu |
| Day 20 to 40 | An old friend may find you |
| Any time | Your origin's old ties come back: an old comrade, a lord who remembers, a debt |
| Before day 60 | You may retrain three skill points in Camp > The Chronicle > Your person |

### Worth doing early

- **Visit landmarks.** They are always shown on the map, and their names tell you which ones you have not explored. Many hold stories, shrines or items.
- **Find the inns.** Six inns on the roads offer rooms and rumours, and quarters where you can leave up to forty men.
- **Hire companions** in the taverns. Their own stories open as you win battles together (section 6).

## 5. The five organisations

Each organisation has five ranks. Your rank follows your standing with it: the second rank comes at standing 20, then 50, 100 and 170. Standing comes from tasks and deeds that suit the organisation. Each rank opens a story of its own.

| Organisation | Where to join | Needs | What it values |
|---|---|---|---|
| Order of the Lantern | The chapter house, in a town's Temple Quarter | Honour 10, renown 150 | Honour, victories over outlaws and raiders, tournament glory |
| Merchants' Guild | The Guild hall, on Guild Row | 1000 denars | Deliveries, courier contracts, workshops you own, paid dues |
| Grey Road | The back alleys of the Low Town | Notoriety 10 | Notoriety, raids, jobs best done at night |
| Wardens' League | The Bounty Clerk, in the tavern | Notoriety under 20, honour not below zero | Keeping the roads: bounties, outlaw prisoners, patrols |
| Free Companies | The Captain of the Free Companies, in the tavern | Nothing | War contracts |

The Grey Road wants notoriety of 10 or more and the Wardens want it under 20, so those two pull in opposite directions.

| Grey Road | Footpad, Cutpurse, Road Captain, Shadow of the Road, Lord of Thieves | Jelkala, Veluca, Shariz |

Members of the Order can also take a vow: poverty, protection or valour. Break a vow and the Order will not forget.

## 6. Your companions

There are sixteen companions, and each has a story in three acts. You start an act by talking to the companion while they ride with you.

| Act | Needs |
|---|---|
| First | 3 battles won together |
| Second | 10 battles won together, and the first act ended well |
| Third | 20 battles won together, and the second act ended well |

Reports > Records of your chronicle > tavern talk shows which acts you can start and where. A companion who fights beside you for twenty-five battles becomes a sworn brother.

Companions judge what you do. Executing prisoners, sacking towns, robbing pilgrims or working for the Grey Road may bring a complaint, or approval from the hard-bitten ones.

## 7. A place in the world

### Serve a king, or his rival

Every kingdom has two lines of stories: four for the sitting ruler and two for the claimant.

- **For the ruler** you must be a vassal of that realm. Ask the ruler and the two lords named below.
- **For the claimant** you must have sworn no oath to the sitting ruler. Ask the claimant.

| Realm | The ruler's line: ask | The claimant's line: ask |
|---|---|---|
| Swadia | King Harlaus, Count Delinard, Count Klargus | Lady Isolla |
| Vaegirs | King Yaroglek, Boyar Naldera, Boyar Vuldrat | Prince Valdym |
| Khergits | Sanjar Khan, Belir Noyan, Tonju Noyan | Dustum Khan |
| Nords | King Ragnar, Jarl Irya, Jarl Aedin | Lethwin Far-Seeker |
| Rhodoks | King Graveth, Count Gutlans, Count Matheas | Lord Kastor |
| Sarranids | Sultan Hakim, Emir Uqais, Emir Hamezan | Arwa the Pearled One |

Kings also ask you to break a named host on the roads once your renown reaches 300, 600 and 1000.

### Other roads

- **Soldier.** Talk to a lord and ask to join his army as a soldier. You rise through six ranks by fighting and by doing duties every few days (watch, forage, scout, baggage). You may take leave, but if you overstay you are a deserter. Your own men wait for you while you serve.
- **Merchant.** Workshops, caravans, the bank, rooms, shops and warehouses, with a weekly budget in Reports. The Merchants' Guild is the natural home.
- **Lord of your own land.** Claim empty land for a settlement at renown 100 for 2000 denars, and grow it from a camp to an outpost and a stone fort. Four ruins can be restored with three loads of tools and 1500 denars. You can also rebuild the ruined keep of Ravenhold.
- **Fief holder.** Set tax rates, raise buildings, appoint household officers, and raise a patrol from the garrison to keep raiders off your land.
- **Raider.** Rob the roads, take black rent from villages and tolls from crossings, and claim Raider's Bluff as a haven. It pays more and costs friends: named hunters ride for you and the kingdoms' Wanted levels rise.
- **King.** A treasury, laws, a royal court, a coronation and a monthly council open once you hold a crown.

### Family

Marry, and family stories begin to arrive by themselves. You may take in a ward for 1000 denars at renown 100. Heirs come of age after 150 days and can be tutored up to five times. If you fall with permadeath on, or choose to pass the mantle, your heir takes your name and keeps your fiefs, companions, army, items and records.

## 8. When the age turns

From the day you chose (day 230 unless you changed it), great crises reshape Calradia one at a time. About ten days before each, signs appear in your log and on your character sheet. In any town, open **News, notices and events > The troubles of the age**.

| Crisis | What it asks of you | Its story begins |
|---|---|---|
| The Long Winter | Feed the hungry with grain | By letter, at Reyvadin |
| The Succession War | Back a claimant with a war chest and your victories | When the claimant you backed sends for you |
| The Comet Year | Read the signs | By letter, at the Astronomer's Tower |
| The Iron Tide | Break the great free companies | By messenger, at Dhirim |

The Black Khergit Horde and the Pale Fever (section 4) are threats of the same size.

You can switch crises, the Horde, the fever and other threats off in Options.

## 9. The 42 Legacies

A Legacy is the great conclusion of a career. When one arrives you may gather your companions and hear their fates, close the chronicle and hear your deeds recounted, or ride on. Only falling in battle with permadeath on ends a game for good.

|---|---|
| The crown | High Monarch of Calradia, The Kingmaker, The People's King, Father of the Realm, Emperor of the Restored Empire, High Chancellor of the Throne, Breaker of Crowns |

| Trade | Merchant Prince, Guildmaster of Calradia, Master of the Roads |

In the game, Reports > Records of your chronicle > Chronicle of Deeds lists the deeds still ahead of you.

## 10. If you are stuck

| The problem | What to do |
|---|---|
| I cannot find a story | Reports > Records of your chronicle > tavern talk names who wants a word, and where. In a town, use Look for somebody who is waiting to be asked. Visit landmarks |
| I lost track of a story | Notes > Quests lists every story in progress |
| An option is greyed | The reason is in brackets after it. Another option on the same page is always open |
| The roads are too dangerous | Keep your company under twelve early on. Switch Hostile wanderers off, or lower the difficulty: Story difficulty never sends hostile wanderers |
| Too much is happening at once | Options can switch off omens, raiders, assassins, the rival, crises, tips, hostile wanderers and walk-in places |
| I was turned away at a castle hall | Commoners are refused until they bribe or talk their way in. Fine clothes count one class higher: Camp > The Chronicle > Your person |
| I am injured | Injuries show on your character sheet. Have a physician, an abbot or a wise woman treat them within 60 days, or they set for good |
| I do not want to walk around towns | Choose Speak with someone without walking about (quick), or turn walk-in places off in Options |

## 11. Battle orders: how to use them

*New in version 11.8. In the game the same is under Notes > Game Concepts > "Reimagined: Battle orders and keys".*

### Giving an order in three steps

1. **Choose who listens.** Press 1-9 for one group (1 infantry, 2 archers, 3 cavalry) or 0 for everybody, exactly as for the old orders.
2. **Open a page.** F5 formations, F6 foot, F7 shot, F8 horse, F9 battle plans. The page shows at the top left of the screen. The battle does not stop.
3. **Press the key of the order** on that page. The page closes, and a line in the message log says what each group now does, or why it cannot do it.

Good to know:

- **F4 is the order card.** It shows every page and what each of your groups is doing right now. Press F4 whenever you are lost.
- A page closes with F4 or Escape, or by itself after fifteen seconds. To change to another page, close this one first.
- F1, F2 and F3 are still the old orders. Right after you press one of them, the next F-key belongs to the old order menu.
- Formations, tactics and plans are for **field battles**. In a siege only your powers (F10) work.

### Formation and tactic

- A **formation** is a shape your men keep. Every man has a place and goes back to it, with shields and the best men in front.
- A **tactic** is what a group does. A tactic given to men without a formation takes the formation it needs.
- **The old orders lead a formed group.** Hold (F1, F1) moves the formation to your flag; groups held at the same flag form up one behind the other. Follow (F1, F2) keeps it behind you. Charge (F1, F3) ends the formation and the tactic and lets the men go.
- **To end only the tactic,** give a formation (F5). **To end everything** for the listening groups: F5, then F11. For the whole army: F11, then F9.
- The same formation again makes a line, a shield wall or a loose order one rank deeper.

### Three ways to begin

- **Hold your ground.** Press 1, then F5 and F6: the infantry forms a shield wall. Press 2, then F7 and F5: the archers shoot in volleys. Press 0, then F1 and F1 where you want the fight: both form up at your flag.
- **Break a line.** Press 3, then F8 and F9: the cavalry rides round the left flank into the enemy's rear and charges his back. As they strike, press 1, then F6 and F5: the infantry advances in order and goes in together.
- **Let a plan lead.** Press F9, then the plan of your own troops' people. It takes the whole army, gives each group its part and tells you each stage as it comes. F11, then F8 ends the plan.

| Page | Then F5 to F11 |
|---|---|
| **F4** Order card | Shows the pages and what each of your groups is doing. F4 again closes it |
| **F5** Formations | F5 line in ranks, F6 shield wall, F7 wedge, F8 square, F9 loose order, F10 column, F11 no formation and no tactic |
| **F6** Foot | F5 advance in order, F6 brace spears, F7 boar's snout, F8 hold the anvil, F9 skirmish, F10 throw, then charge |
| **F7** Shot | F5 volleys, F6 hold fire till close, F7 arrow storm, F8 skirmish, F9 screen in front of the foot, F10 behind pavises |
| **F8** Horse | F5 lance wedge, F6 strike and re-form, F7 feigned flight, F8 riding circle, F9 sweep to the left, F10 sweep to the right, F11 stand in reserve |
| **F9** Battle plans | F5 Three Battles (Swadian), F6 The Ambush Regiment (Vaegir), F7 The Feigned Flight (Khergit), F8 Shield Wall and Snout (Nord), F9 Hedge and Bolt (Rhodok), F10 Attack and Withdraw (Sarranid), F11 Hammer and Anvil |
| **F10** Your powers | F5 battle cry, F6 rally, F7 surge, F8 resupply, F9 loose!, F10 your own power, F11 lock shields (in a siege: boiling oil) |
| **F11** Signals and rules | F5 release the reserve, F6 all: re-form on me, F7 all: fall back to the start line, F8 end the battle plan, F9 end every formation and tactic, F10 captains use tactics: on or off, F11 pace of the plans |

### What each formation does

| Formation | What the men do | Use it when |
|---|---|---|
| Line in ranks | Stand in ranks facing the enemy | You want order and a clear front |
| Shield wall | Stand close, shields in front, and move at a walk. Men with a shield take half the damage from arrows and bolts and less from blows | Arrows are falling, or you must hold. At least a third of the group must carry shields |
| Wedge | Form a point with the best man at the tip | You mean to break through a line |
| Square | Face outward on all sides and move slowly | Horsemen are all around you |
| Loose order | Stand far apart. A quarter of the shots from far off miss | You are being shot at from a distance and no charge is coming |
| Column | March three abreast, a little faster | You need to move, not fight |

### What each tactic does

| Foot (F6) | What the men do | Needs |
|---|---|---|
| Advance in order | Walk at the enemy keeping their ranks, halt a short way off, then go in together | |
| Brace spears | Stand still with spears set. A horse that rides into them is badly hurt | Long spears for at least a quarter of the group |
| Boar's snout | Run in as a wedge and strike hard for a short time. If they do not break through, they tire | |
| Hold the anvil | Stand and hold: they do not chase, and a feigned flight does not draw them out | |
| Skirmish | Keep their distance: give ground when the enemy comes on, come back when he goes | |
| Throw, then charge | Wait until the enemy is near, throw everything, then charge | Throwing weapons, or it is only a late charge |

| Shot (F7) | What the men do | Needs |
|---|---|---|
| Volleys | Hold their fire and loose together | Bows, crossbows or throwing weapons for at least half the group |
| Hold fire till close | Do not shoot until the enemy is near, then hit harder for a while | The same |
| Arrow storm | Shoot as fast as they can, less truly, for a time; then their arms are tired | The same |
| Skirmish | Stay out of reach, shooting, and never let themselves be caught | |
| Screen in front of the foot | Stand ahead of your foot, then fall back behind the line when the enemy closes | |
| Behind pavises | Shoot from cover: men with a shield load faster and take half the damage from arrows and bolts | The same, and shields for at least a third |

| Horse (F8) | What the men do | Needs |
|---|---|---|
| Lance wedge | Trot in a wedge, gallop the last stretch and strike very hard on impact | Horses |
| Strike and re-form | Charge, fight for a moment, ride back, form up and charge again | Horses |
| Feigned flight | Ride at the enemy, turn and run. Enemy groups may chase; your riders then turn on men strung out behind them | |
| Riding circle | Horse archers ride a ring in front of the enemy, shooting, and keep their distance | Horses, and bows for at least half the group |
| Sweep to the left or right | Ride a wide arc round the flank into the enemy's rear, then charge his back | |
| Stand in reserve | Wait behind your line and take no orders until you release them (F11, then F5). Fresh men then hit harder for a short time | |

### The battle plans (F9)

| Plan | What your army does |
|---|---|
| Three Battles (Swadian) | The shot goes forward to open the fight while the horse waits behind. The horse then goes in as a lance wedge, striking and re-forming. Then the foot advances in order, and at the end every man goes forward |
| The Ambush Regiment (Vaegir) | The foot holds as the anvil with the shot behind it and the horse waits in reserve; when the enemy is fighting the centre, the reserve sweeps into his rear |
| The Feigned Flight (Khergit) | Horse archers ride the circle, then feign flight; when they turn, everybody charges |
| Shield Wall and Snout (Nord) | The shield wall advances with the shot behind it, throws at close range, then forms the boar's snout and goes in |
| Hedge and Bolt (Rhodok) | Spears braced in front, the shot behind pavises, the horse in reserve. When the enemy is spent, the hedge rises and pushes |
| Attack and Withdraw (Sarranid) | The horse strikes and re-forms while the archers screen; after several strikes everybody charges |
| Hammer and Anvil | The foot holds, the horse sweeps into the rear |

Any commander can give any plan. A plan needs the right troops: without horse there is no hammer.

### Their own way of war

Troops do a thing better when it is their own people's way: Nord foot in the shield wall and the boar's snout; Rhodok foot with braced spears, in the square and behind pavises; Swadian horse in the lance wedge; Khergit horse in the riding circle, the feigned flight and the sweeps; Sarranid horse striking and re-forming; Vaegir archers in volleys and the arrow storm. When a group takes an order that is its own, the message says "(Their own way of war.)".

### When an order does not work

| What you see | Why, and what to do |
|---|---|
| "No group is listening." | Choose a group first: 1-9, or 0 for everybody |
| "They have no horses for that." | The lance wedge, strike and re-form and the riding circle are for mounted men. Choose your cavalry |
| "Too few of them carry a shield / a long spear / a bow..." | The group lacks the arms for that order. Choose another group or another order |
| "Formations and tactics are for the open field." | You are in a siege or another fight where only the powers work |
| The old order menu opened instead | You pressed F1, F2 or F3 first. Press Escape and start again |
| A group ignores you | It stands in reserve: release it with F11, then F5 |
| The page does not show | The orders still work; the message log names every key of the page |

### The captains

Enemy lords and the lords at your side use the plans of their people when they have the troops for them. Outlaws fight in the way of the land they come from; a band of fewer than twelve just fights, and so does a host with too few of the right troops. F11, then F10 switches this off or on, and the game remembers it. F11, then F11 slows the plans down if they move too fast for you.

### The powers and their own keys

The powers are on the F10 page, and each also has its own key:

| Key | What it does |
|---|---|
| B | Battle cry: your men strike harder and some of the enemy may break (once per battle) |
| N | Rally: steadies wavering men and heals your companions a little |
| K | Surge: your men press forward faster for a short time (once per battle) |
