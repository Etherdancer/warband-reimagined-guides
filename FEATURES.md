# Warband Reimagined - Full feature list

*Everything in the mod, grouped by category. Back to the Steam Workshop page for install steps and feedback.*

## Your Story
### Choose Who You Were

- After the opening, tell the chroniclers who you were before Calradia. Each origin starts you differently:
- - A free adventurer (the Native start).
- - The captain of a small mercenary band - eight men and some renown.
- - The heir of a merchant house - gold, fine goods and a Guild charter.
- - A hunted outlaw - cutthroats, notoriety and friends on the Grey Road.
- - A pilgrim - piety, the trust of the common folk and the notice of the Order of the Dawn.
- - A disgraced noble - a famous name and a bank loan due in sixty days.
- - A veteran soldier - old comrades, skills and weapon training.

### Your Standing In Calradia

- Three new reputation tracks beside Renown and Honour:
- - Commoners' Trust - how the common folk see you. Raised by protecting and helping villagers, fair judgements and charity; lowered by raids, robbery and extortion.
- - Notoriety - your criminal fame. Opens doors in the underworld, slowly fades with time.
- - Piety - earned through prayer, charity and pilgrimage.
- A status ladder from Nobody through Sellsword, Captain, Warlord, Vassal, Great Lord and Marshal up to Sovereign.
- Everything is visible in the new camp menu "Reimagined: standing, journal and options".

### Milestones And Titles

- 144 milestones: battles won, foes felled, tournaments, quests, renown, honour, wealth, army size, companions, fiefs, marriage, crowns, workshops, exploration, contracts, reputation... and a few darker ones such as "Village Burner" and "Headsman", and great ones like "Living Legend" for completing the story arcs.
- Each milestone earns a title in your Journal of Deeds and a real reward: attribute or skill increases, renown, gold, weapon proficiency.
- A "Goals Ahead" page always shows what you could achieve next.
- Survey the realms: a live table of every kingdom - its ruler, towns, castles, lords, wars and how its ruler regards you.
- The Chronicle of Deeds: a scrolling screen listing every milestone in the game - the ones you have earned and the ones still waiting - and every ending you have reached.
- Also: win tournaments in five different towns, or visit every town in Calradia.

### Statistics Of Your Chronicle

- A full statistics screen (Records) tracks your whole playthrough: battles won and lost, winning streaks, foes you killed or knocked senseless with your own hands, kills from the saddle, headshots, enemy lords struck down, the largest army you ever faced, men and companions lost, battle orders given and enemies put to flight, leagues travelled, towns and villages visited, the most gold you ever held, income from your holdings, caravan profits, gambling wins and losses, prisoners freed or sold, pockets picked, pilgrims robbed, prayers led, days served as a soldier, duties and promotions - and your score so far.
- Fifteen new achievements are built on them: Slayer, Butcher of Calradia, Marksman, Bane of Lords, Unbroken, Wayfarer, Walked the World, Titanic Clash, Voice of Command, Liberator, Lord of Coin, Light Fingers, Friend of the Countryside, Dutiful Soldier and Reader of Omens.

### Name Your Company

- Give your company a name of its own, shown on the map (Options).

### Story: Blood And Ashes

- A personal story that begins in your first week: the raider who burned the village where you grew up has been seen again. Follow the trail from a Rivacheg inn to a crossroads gallows and a Bariyye fence, storm his lair at Raider's Bluff - then kill him, spare him, or make him serve you.

### Story: The Emperor's Regalia

- A scholar of Zendar asks you to find the six lost regalia of the Calradic Empire. Each lies at a hidden site and each is won differently: a fight with tomb robbers, a sage's examination, a bank vault (buy or burgle), a feat of strength, a rival band of treasure-hunters, and a test of faith.
- When you hold all six, decide their fate: proclaim the Restored Calradic Empire, crown your liege as High Monarch, destroy them forever, or sell them to the merchant princes of Zendar.

### Story: The Sea-King's Hoard

- Once your name is known, an old sailor in Wercheg tells of Harald Sea-King's buried hoard. Search the Broken Lighthouse for his map, buy a Halmar skald's saga, drive the false heirs from Raider's Bluff and dig beneath the Standing Stones. Keep the gold, share it with your men, or give it back to the gods.

### Story: The Barefoot Prophet

- A preacher in Uxkhal tells the peasants that the land belongs to those who plough it. Protect him - free his followers, guard his great sermon and become the Sword of the Poor - or take the lords' silver and hunt his zealots down.

### Story: The Lost Legion

- The last imperial army in Calradia vanished with its golden eagle. Follow the Ninth Legion's trail from an old watchtower to a brigands' den, the legion's graves and the fighting pits of the Old Arena - then raise the legion anew, give the eagle to your king, or lay it in a cathedral.

### A Rival Of Your Own

- Once your name is known, a rival mercenary captain swears to see your head on a spear. He hunts you across Calradia, escapes each defeat and comes back with a stronger company. Break him three times, then decide his fate: finish him, take him into your service, or sell him to his creditors.

### The Notice Board - Repeatable Contracts

- Every town has a notice board with three contracts, refreshed every few days:
- - Bounties on named outlaw bands, spawned on the map for you to hunt.
- - Deserter hunts.
- - Road patrols: defeat a number of bandit bands before the deadline.
- - Goods deliveries to another town.
- - Sealed letters carried to another town.
- - Captives to free from outlaw bands.
- - Famine relief: grain for a hungry town.
- - Scouting missions: count the walls and garrison of a distant fortress.
- Rewards scale with your renown; failing a deadline costs you standing.

## A Living World
### The Turning Year

- Calradia has seasons. Each year of 120 days passes through spring, summer, autumn and winter.
- - Spring: your villages prosper as the fields are sown.
- - Summer: the campaigning season; your men drill in the long days.
- - Autumn: harvest dues from your villages.
- - Winter: the cold wears on your men - unless you carry furs.
- Each season brings a great event to a random town for twelve days:
- - Spring: the Great Tournament - win the lists there for a great prize.
- - Summer: the Great Fair - goods are cheap in the host town.
- - Autumn: the Harvest Festival - feast and give thanks with the town.
- - Winter: the Midwinter Feast - bring gifts and win the favour of a whole realm's lords.
- Eight seasonal road events: spring floods and fairs, summer drought and wildfire, the autumn harvest and hunt, winter blizzards and wolves.


### Omens

- Now and then the sky itself speaks: comets, eclipses, blood moons and a white stag at dawn - each with its effect on your men.

### World Events

- The Black Khergit Horde: rumours from the steppe, then a great invasion. Five named warbands roam Calradia burning villages. Break them yourself and be remembered as the Saviour of Calradia.
- The Pale Fever: a plague that spreads from town to town along the trade roads. Help stricken towns (at some risk to your men) until you understand the fever well enough to cure it.
- Or betray Calradia: an adventurer sworn to no king may ride to the Khan and offer his sword. Every kingdom turns against you, but every village the Horde burns pays you a share - and thirty burned villages make you the Scourge of Calradia.
- A busier world: patrols of the Order of the Dawn ride the roads hunting bandits, and bands of pilgrims travel from town to town. Guard them - or rob them, and see how quickly word spreads. Cut down the Order's knights and the Order will not forget.
- Stop and talk to them: give pilgrims alms or pray with them (or shake them down), ride a day with an Order patrol, ask for news of the roads, or - as a senior brother - ask the patrol for squires.
- The Bandit King: from day 100, a great host of outlaws gathers under one master and burns its way from village to village until someone breaks it.
- Famine: some winters, a kingdom starves for a month. Grain doubles in price in its towns - sell yours at a fair price and be blessed, or at famine prices and be rich.
- Both world events can be switched off in the options.

### Lords Are Mortal

- Lords of Calradia can now die of illness, and their lands pass to their king. (Can be switched off.)
- Pass judgement on captive lords: release them on their oath and win a friend, or execute them and be feared and hated across Calradia.

### The Wandering Master

- Somewhere on the roads of Calradia walks an old teacher who has taught princes and thieves. Find him, and for a fee he will teach you tactics, surgery, training, pathfinding, persuasion or siege engineering.

## War
### A Soldier's Career

- Offer your sword to a friendly lord and serve in his host as a common soldier. Your own men wait for you in reserve.
- March and fight with his army, earn weekly pay, and rise through six ranks from Recruit to Captain of the Host.
- Leave with an honourable discharge after 30 days - or desert, and live with the consequences.
- Enlist by talking to the lord himself, or from the encounter menu.
- Duties every few days - night watch, foraging, scouting ahead, guarding the baggage - test your skills and earn merit towards promotion.
- Each promotion brings new kit from the quartermaster: spear and shield, mail and helmet, sword, a horse, and finally a warhorse and lance.
- Weekly pay and rations, a bonus for every victory, and a week's leave after two weeks of service - but come back, or be counted a deserter.

### Before The Battle

- Commander's options before any fight: give a rousing speech, challenge the enemy champion to single combat, send skirmishers ahead, or lead your men in prayer. Success and failure both change the battle to come.

### In The Heat Of Battle

- Battle Cry (B, once per battle): your men within earshot strike 20% harder for thirty seconds, and wavering enemies nearby may break.
- Rally (N, once per battle): fleeing men near you turn and fight again, steadier and quicker for twenty seconds.
- Champion's fury: every fifth enemy you cut down yourself fires up the men around you for fifteen seconds.
- If you fall, your men see it - and they break far sooner.
- Headshots: a blow or arrow that lands at head height deals half again as much damage - to your enemies, and to you.
- Night and weather: at night every archer's aim suffers; in winter, deep snow slows every step.
- Boiling oil (O, once per siege assault): stand on the wall above the attackers and scald everyone below you.
- All of this works in sieges, hideout raids and village fights too.
- Last stand: once per battle, when you are close to death, you find the strength to strike a quarter harder.
- Shield Wall (H): men with shields near you lock together - slower, but they take a quarter less damage. Re-form after a minute.
- Archer Volley (J, once per battle): your archers shoot faster and truer.
- Lances and spears strike a fifth harder from horseback.
- Kill an enemy lord and his men waver; lose a companion and your men fight for vengeance. Veteran soldiers (level 25+) never rout.
- Charge (K, once per battle): your whole line surges forward.
- Resupply (L, once per battle): the baggage boys run fresh ammunition to the men near you. A rally now also bolsters your wounded companions.
- Morale and rout: armies no longer fight to the last man. When most of a side has fallen, its soldiers begin to break and flee the field - yours included, unless you rally them.

### After The Battle

- Veterans who have followed you through many campaigns may ask to be honoured: make one a sergeant, send him home with a purse, or knight him before the whole company.
- After a large victory, decide what happens on the field: bury the dead of both sides, share a bonus with your men, or strip the fallen of everything they carried. (Can be switched off.)
- Wounds that linger: a bad beating can leave you with a lasting wound that slows you until it heals - or until a town physician treats it.
- Guard a village for half a day. Usually it passes quietly; sometimes the raiders come.


### Siege Warfare

- Plan your sieges with your captains: blockade every road so hunger weakens the garrison day by day, pay agents to find a traitor inside, send saboteurs over the walls at night, or strip the countryside for supplies.
- Dig a mine under the walls (needs an engineer): bring a tower down on the garrison - or the tunnel down on your own miners.
- 8 siege camp events: dysentery, night sallies, deserters from the walls, poisoned streams, refugees driven out of the gates, fires, boredom and fraternisation.

### New Soldiers

- Seven new troop lines, all in vanilla equipment, each tied to a path:
- - Order of the Dawn: Squire, Knight and Paladin of the Dawn.
- - The Grey Road: Cutthroat, Blade and Shadow of the Road - quick fighters with throwing knives.
- - The Merchants' Guild: Caravan Guard and Guild Man-at-Arms.
- - The faithful: Pilgrim Zealots and Crusaders of the Roads.
- - Your own settlements: Settler Militia and Frontier Guards.
- - The Ninth Legion reborn: Legion Recruits, Legionaries and Legion Veterans with shield and javelin.
- - Steppe Exiles and Exile Riders - horse archers of Baheshtur's clan.

## Life On The Road
### Camp Life

- Camp activities, once a day each: hunt for game, send out foragers, drill the men, play dice, lead prayers or write in your chronicle.
- Spar with your companions, or send scouts to find outlaw hideouts nearby and mark them on your map.
- Decide the fate of your common prisoners: set them free and be blessed for it, or sell them to passing slavers.
- Collect pilgrim's tokens from the keepers of Calradia's holy places. Five tokens make you a Relic Keeper.
- Beaten outlaws sometimes carry treasure maps. Follow one to the landmark it marks and dig.

### Your Name Has Weight

- High Commoners' Trust brings village lads to your banner every week. Piety steadies your men. Honour wins the respect of lords. And a notorious member of the Grey Road gets a weekly cut.
- But notoriety has a price: bounty hunters will come for you.

### Talking To Lords

- New things to say to the lords of Calradia: present a gift to win their favour, ask a lord who likes you to lend you men from his host, or ask what he really thinks of his peers - friend, rival or bitter enemy.
- Challenge a friendly lord to a bout in the lists: win and your name goes round the halls; lose with grace and he still thinks better of you.

### Talking To Village Elders

- Give a gift to a village, or have your men drill its militia for a day.

## Money, Land And Power
### Money And Property

- The Bank of Calradia: deposit your savings for weekly interest, or take a loan against your renown - and repay it before the bankers remember your name for the wrong reasons.
- Commission a bronze statue of yourself in a town square, once you are famous enough and rich enough.
- Everything a town offers beyond Native - guilds, bank, property, taverns, baths, surgeons, library, church and work - is gathered under "Town affairs", to keep the town menu tidy.
- Buy townhouses in any town and let them for weekly rent that grows with the town's prosperity.
- Outfit up to three trade caravans of your own (from 50 renown). They bring back weekly profits - when raiders don't take them.
- Your companions walk the town streets at your side. (Can be switched off.)
- Light fingers: walking the streets of a town, a thief (or a member of the Grey Road) can try to pick a pocket once per visit (U). Get caught and the town will remember.
- Tavern games in every town: arm-wrestling, drinking contests and cards.
- More to do in towns: soak your wounds in the bathhouse, pay the barber-surgeons to tend your wounded, study in the library, give alms at the church door, or invest in the market for a weekly dividend.
- Keep a trade ledger: every town you visit, you note its prices. The ledger shows where each good is cheapest and dearest among the towns you have seen.
- Down on your luck? Hire out your company for a day's labour at the docks of a town or in a village's fields. Honest pay - though a famous captain may be mocked for it.

### Found Your Own Settlement

- Six wild sites lie unclaimed across Calradia: Wolf Valley, Greyfield, Ravens' Ford, Stonewater, Eagle Rock and Palm Hollow. Claim one and grow it from a camp to an outpost to a stone fort.
- Your settlements store up income and settlers' sons eager to join you until you come to collect them. Raiders may strike camps and outposts; only stone walls keep them out. Raiders are seen marching on your settlements - catch them on the road and the settlers will thank you.
- Build a chapel, a market square and a training yard in your outposts, and breed warhorses in the stables of your forts. Rest safely within your own fort.

### Ruling Your Fiefs

- Set a tax rate for each fief: low, normal, high or extortionate. Income rises or falls, and so do prosperity and your people's trust.
- Build 11 new structures: granary, orchard, shrine, palisade, market hall, guild house, hospital, courthouse, training yard, stud farm and barracks. Each has a real weekly effect.
- Rise through noble titles as your lands grow: Knight of the Realm, Lord, Baron, Count, Duke, King and High Monarch. Counts and above gain renown every week.
- Rule badly and your people may revolt: when the common folk distrust you, a fief can rise up, burn the tax rolls and take to the hills.
- Appoint household officers once you hold land: a Steward, a Master-at-Arms, a Chaplain, a Physician, a Herald and a Castellan. Each draws a weekly wage and does real work for you.


### The Royal Court

- Once you wear a crown, rule by decree. Eight royal decrees, each with a weekly cost and effect: the Crown Tithe, Royal Road Wardens, a Standing Army, the Charter of Town Liberties, Patronage of the Faith, the Code of Honour, Martial Law and Open Markets. Some cannot stand together.
- A crown makes enemies: rival kings may send hired assassins after you.
- Hold a great royal feast for your vassals, or issue yourself a royal pardon for past crimes - at a price.
- Diplomacy: send envoys bearing gifts to foreign rulers, propose peace for a tribute, demand tribute from a weaker foe, seal a 60-day non-aggression pact, or declare war. A king who holds twice the towns of a foe at war can demand that it bow as a tributary and pay tribute every week.

## Organisations
### The Three Organisations

- The Order of the Dawn: a knightly order sworn to guard the roads. Rise from Aspirant to Grandmaster through vigils, tithes, tournament glory and victories over outlaws. Senior members can bring brother-knights into their company.
- Brothers of the Order may swear a vow - of Poverty, Protection or Valour - that brings weekly rewards, until the day they break it.
- The Merchants' Guild: buy a charter, run trading ventures, deliver contracts and own workshops to rise from Associate to Guildmaster of Calradia. Senior members draw a weekly stipend.
- The Grey Road: the underworld. Smuggle, extort, fence stolen goods and raid your way from Footpad to Lord of Thieves. Hire cutthroats, or have friends in high places make your records disappear.

### Great Deeds Of The Organisations

- Each organisation has its own finale for its senior members:
- - The Last Vigil (Order of the Dawn): recover the Order's first banner, defend its people and keep the vigil to be acclaimed Grandmaster.
- - The Great Heist (Grey Road): recruit a locksmith, scout the treasury of Veluca and rob it blind - or betray the Grey Road to the Order.
- - Coin of the Realm (Merchants' Guild): corner the velvet trade, lend gold to a king and found the Bank of Calradia.

## Guide, Journal And Options

- Active affairs: one screen listing every story and quest in progress and where to go next, plus standing oaths, memberships, threats.
- In-game Guide: eight short pages explaining the systems, battle keys, reputation, money, organisations, endings and career.
- More options: switch off omens, settlement raiders, assassins, the vengeful rival, or the helpful tips.
- A short welcome tip on your first day points you to Camp > Reimagined.

## Difficulty
Five tiers, chosen when the chronicle begins and changeable in Options: **Story** (very forgiving), **Gentle** (really easy), **Standard** (the default), **Hard** and **Brutal**.

- Gold rewards from quests, events and prizes: Story 300%, Gentle 200%, Standard 100%, Hard 80%, Brutal 65%.
- How often raiders, assassins, fief riots and arrests come, and how many men your rival brings: Story 8%, Gentle 25%, Standard 100%, Hard 150%, Brutal 200%.
- How long low morale and unpaid wages are tolerated before mutiny and desertion.
- How early each side routs in battle (on Gentle your men hold to 14% and the enemy breaks at 55%).
