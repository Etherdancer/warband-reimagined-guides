# Warband Reimagined - Full feature list

*Everything in version 11, grouped by theme. Back to the [README](README.md), the [changelog](CHANGELOG.md) or the [testing guide](TESTING.md).*

**New game required.** Old saves do not work with this version.

**Light on any PC.** Only Native's own models, textures, scenes, sounds and music are used. The one new picture is the title logo.

## 1. Stories and quests

The heart of the mod: **372 stories**, every one on the same story engine, so they all behave the same way.

- **Choices with reasons.** An option you cannot take yet is shown greyed, with the reason in brackets (a skill, a rank, an item, a friend, a trait).
- **An open path at every step.** There is always a way forward that needs no special skill and no gold, though it may cost you something else.
- **Many endings.** Each story has several endings, including failing it. Each ending changes the world: gold and renown are the small part; people, places, laws, friendships and rumours change too.
- **Something to do in every story.** On every way to a good ending you catch, follow, carry, hold, watch, free, fight, escort or ride: a clever answer can help, but it never replaces the deed. Many stories also send you out of town to a village or a far place, with riders waiting on the road.
- **Many kinds of step.** Talk to someone, go to a place, wait out the days, face a trial, sit on a council that votes, escort someone, chase a runner, hunt a band across the map, or walk into a scene where you fight, search, sneak or hold a line.
- **Consequences everywhere.** Honour, trust, notoriety, piety, villages' prosperity, lords' relations, companions' opinions, the law, the organisations and the rumours in the taverns all react. Stories you finish are listed by name in your Chronicle of Deeds.
- **Hunts that vary.** Bounties, bandit-leader hunts, renegade hunts, the marshal's calls, courier runs and the sheriff's jobs have a twist picked by chance when the work is done.
- **Quests come to you.** Offers come from taverners, elders, lords, abbots, harbourmasters and dozens of other people. Some start on their own: a war story in wartime, a festival story while the festival is on.

### The families of stories

- **Side quests** (dozens): the miller's runaway daughter, the forged charter, the poet's debt, the horse thieves, the sword in the well, the stolen relic, the ferrymen's feud, the haunted mill and many more, in every culture.
- **Village chains:** six long stories of six steps, one in each culture's heartland.
- **Personal stories of the townsfolk** (42): smiths, horse dealers, moneylenders, notaries, reeves, millers, sheriffs, abbots, almoners, harbourmasters and more. How each ends changes what that person can do for you.
- **Road stories** (25): work for a rider, on either side of the law (see sections 6 and 7).
- **Great stories** (section 2), **companion stories** (section 3), **kingdom and organisation lines** (section 4), and the **story lines** of section 5.
- **Repeatable work:** the notice board in every town, bounties on moving fugitives, bandit-leader hunts, courier runs, the sheriff's jobs, the marshal's calls in wartime.

## 2. The great stories

Long, branching stories with their own rewards.

- **Blood and Ashes:** who paid Vargan the Red to burn Old Ruvar? Follow the trail, then decide his fate.
- **The Emperor's Regalia:** six chapters, six lost regalia, and a last chase. Proclaim an empire, crown a king, destroy them, or sell them.
- **The Black Khergit Horde:** a war in four acts. Break the warbands, raise the kings, hold the markets, or ride with the Khan.
- **The Pale Fever:** an investigation across the plague towns, a Church decree, a quarantine, a trial and a burning.
- **Salt and the Sword:** a dead reeve, a hidden ledger, a miners' march.
- **The Wolf Priest:** a cult in the Redwood camp, a village under siege.
- **The Drowned Crown:** a lost prince, a false heir, a waterfront council.
- **The Scholar's Codex:** three pages, three ways to get them, one book that names the Orb of Dawn.
- **The Last Vigil, The Great Heist, Coin of the Realm:** the finales of the Order of the Lantern, the Grey Road and the Merchants' Guild.
- **The Sea-King's Hoard, The Barefoot Prophet, The Lost Legion, The Bells of Saint Ymra, The Justiciar's Court, The Cartographer's Map.**
- **A story inside each world crisis:** the Long Winter, the Succession War, the Comet Year and the Iron Tide.

## 3. Your companions

- Sixteen companions, each with a **three-act story** of their own: Borcha, Marnid, Ymira, Rolf, Baheshtur, Firentis, Deshavi, Matheld, Alayen, Bunduk, Katrin, Jeremus, Nizar, Lezalit, Artimenner and Klethi.
- Their fates are read by the finale. Companions judge your deeds, have ambitions, and can say how the company and you strike them.
- **The Lieutenant's Banner** (optional, off by default): a companion leads twenty to sixty of your men as a second party. It rides at your side or patrols around a town, draws wages, and can be recalled at camp. If it falls, the companion turns up in a tavern twenty days later.

## 4. Kingdoms and organisations

- **Six kingdoms, each with two rulers' lines.** For King Harlaus or Lady Isolla in Swadia, Yaroglek or Valdym in Vaegir lands, Sanjar or Dustum among the Khergits, Ragnar or Lethwin among the Nords, Graveth or Kastor among the Rhodoks, Hakim or Arwa among the Sarranids. Each has votes, feasts, tournaments, ferries, passes and secrets, and each ends with an honour.
- **Five organisations with rank ladders:** the Order of the Lantern, the Merchants' Guild, the Grey Road, the Wardens' League and the Free Companies. Each rank is earned through a story; each ladder ends in a finale.
- Organisation vows, tithes, stipends, troop lines and the Hooded Council inside the Grey Road.

## 5. Story lines

- **The faith:** a schism over the Grey Father's rite, the hedge priests' licence, the abbots' chapter, the standing stones, a forty-day vow, the road to Hierophant.
- **The map's places:** inns, clan camps, river crossings, burial mounds, hidden places, a quarry, a wolf-pack, each with a keeper and a story.
- **Family:** the spouse's kin, a rival at the feast, an office for your spouse, the heir's tutor, a ward's origin, the heir's first command.
- **Your rival:** where he came from, his lieutenants, his offer.
- **Ravenhold:** the deed, the well, the master-at-arms, the falconer, the siege.
- **The calendar:** a story during each season's great event (the tournament, the fair, the harvest, midwinter).
- **Crime and the law:** a chain gang, a confession, a pardon, a slate to wipe clean.
- **War service:** a sergeant's duty, deserters, a night watch, a discharge.
- **Trade:** a wagon, a herd, parcels, a room, a shop, a warehouse.
- **Your past:** each origin has old ties that come back (an old comrade, a lord who remembers, a debt, a squire, a road you left behind).

## 6. The free road

New in version 11: a living map for players who want to ride and make their own way, with or without the stories.

### Who rides the roads

- **Traders and travellers:** Guild convoys, horse traders, drovers, treasure hunters (the Diggers), pilgrims, a reliquary procession, minstrels, tinkers, envoys, settlers and refugees.
- **Knights and sellswords:** the Errantry, a champion's retinue, banner-seekers, and four named Free Companies that take war contracts.
- **The law and the crown:** road patrols and the king's silver wagon for every kingdom, the Wardens' Riders, the Order's patrols and its Column.
- **The clans:** four clan warbands around their camps. They answer to their goodwill towards you.
- **Outlaws and worse:** slavers' coffles, smuggling trains, and later Sea-Wolves, Black Felt Riders, exiled lords with warbands, a Pretender's Host and the Outlaw King.
- The early game stays gentle. Hostile bands start far away and leave a company of fewer than twelve alone unless provoked.

### Meeting them

- **Every party is met by talk.** Trade with it, escort it, duel its champion, learn from a master, hire from it, ride with it, or rob it.
- **Every attack line names its cost before you act:** the relations, honour and Wanted level you will pay.
- **Road prices:** Guild convoys and horse traders buy and sell at their own prices.
- **Finds:** a robbed procession's reliquary can go back to the Faith or to a fence. The Diggers buy the imperial curios you find in old places.
- Ride with the Errantry to a festival and two of its knights enter the lists there.

### Rich and strong by riding

- **Purses** from every band you break, and **town bounties** for the hostile ones.
- **Captains' prizes:** 25 named arms and armour taken from the captains of the road.
- **A master smith's masterwork forge:** tokens from broken bands buy twelve masterwork pieces.
- **A larger company:** every ten parties broken adds a man to your company's limit, up to twenty.
- **Ransom** for captains at the ransom broker, the remnants of a broken host to take into your company, and a weekly stipend once a hundred parties are broken.
- **Five milestones:** Road-Breaker, Hammer of the Roads, Terror of the Roads, Collector, and Warden of Six Realms.

### Your own patrols

- Raise a patrol from a fief's garrison, give it orders and pay its wages. It runs down small bands and keeps raiders away from your land.
- Ravenhold and your own outposts can post a household patrol.
- As a king, the Road Wardens' Charter puts riders on your realm's roads.

### Roads that change

- **War** doubles the realms' road patrols and thins the Guild convoys. **Feast weeks** fill the roads with pilgrims.
- A claimant's host may seize a river crossing. Lords pay to have it retaken.
- Clans lend a warband to a friend, and raid a realm at war with yours when their goodwill is high.
- Report a band to the Wardens' Riders. The Order pays a writ for raiders broken near a monastery.

### Road work on the right side of the law

- Hunt a band (paid by its strength), guard the king's silver, a Guild convoy or a relic's procession, recover robbed silver, retake a crossing.
- Kings ask for a host to be broken once your renown is high enough.

### Seeing it all

- **Reports > The roads:** who rides, where they were last seen, your trophies, your patrols, and a chronicle of your first victories.
- Start rules: **Life on the roads** (quiet, lively, crowded) and **Hostile wanderers** (on or off). Story difficulty never sends hostile wanderers.

## 7. The raider road

For those who want to be the villain. It pays more, and it costs friends.

- **The Black Ledger:** notoriety ranks from Footpad to Reaver, Dread Captain and Scourge of the Roads, each with perks. Bandits stop attacking a Reaver and will ride with him for coin.
- **Raider's Bluff:** a haven to claim and build up (longhouse, stockade, hall) with a fence, a slaver's pen, recruits, a hoard, a lookout, and a place to lie low.
- **Black rent** from villages and **tolls** from river crossings you seize.
- **The Black Board:** work from three Reaver Captains (take a wagon, run off a herd, ambush the riders, burn for a paymaster, spring a chain gang).
- **People:** a Lookout in three inns, a Slaver in the Sultanate's Low Towns.
- **The price:** six named hunters ride for you, a hunter can shut the Bluff, robbed goods draw the gate search, and the kingdoms' Wanted levels rise.
- **Ways out and up:** the Outlaw King's offer, a Wardens' amnesty, and a Legacy of its own, Dread of the Roads.

## 8. Choose who you were

Character creation runs in one flow: father, youth, adulthood, why you left, what you became, where your story begins, and the rules of your chronicle.

- **Seven origins**, each with its own opening, kit and old ties: free adventurer, mercenary captain, merchant heir, hunted outlaw, pilgrim, disgraced noble, veteran soldier. Or begin landless, with a village, as a sworn vassal or as the ruler of a town.
- **Rules you set:** when world crises come, total war, bandit numbers, life on the roads, permadeath, five difficulty tiers (Story, Gentle, Standard, Hard, Brutal), changeable later.
- **Character export and import:** carry your character (looks, skills, gold, and your Reimagined record) between games, or import a plain Warband character.
- An optional prologue at Zendar.

## 9. Your standing

- **Reputation tracks** beside Renown and Honour: Commoners' Trust, Notoriety and Piety.
- Social class and prestige; a status ladder from Nobody to Sovereign.
- **Twenty traits** that grow from what you actually do, each grade giving a skill point.
- **One-screen character sheet** (Reports > View character report): every standing value, what it does for you now, what the next step brings, with the law, organisations, house, holdings, the road, friends, enemies, age, traits, and your stories.
- **157 milestones** with real rewards, **42 Legacies** (the great conclusions of a career) with epilogues, a **Chronicle of Deeds** (milestones, Legacies, every story you have ended and how, the people you have met) and a statistics screen with achievements.
- Optional permadeath, with your heir carrying on.

## 10. People and places

- **45 kinds of people** (smiths, horse dealers, moneylenders, notaries, millers, reeves, sheriffs, heralds, abbots, almoners, harbourmasters and more): each has a name, a face and clothes that fit the culture of their town, and remembers whether you have met.
- **Walk into places:** markets, Low Town lanes, guild halls, churches, drill yards, castle halls, village greens, taverns and the mod's map sites; each person stands at the post of their trade. The map sites and the stories' open ground are 37 areas in real locations, each on the land of the map around it (grass, steppe, snow or desert). Harbour towns have a waterfront with a pier and a moored ship.
- **Work and favours:** 32 kinds of people offer work in their trade; do their jobs three times and they offer a lasting favour.
- **Voices:** people answer in the voice of their culture. A person who gave you a story greets you differently once it ended well or badly. Lords tell you what people say of you and what they think of their king. Companions tell you how the company strikes them.
- **Town life:** lessons, drill, feasts, songs, a cellar ring, jousts, dice, and real arena fights; quarters of each town to walk; "ask around" for who is in town.

## 11. A living world

- **Seasons:** spring sowing, summer campaigns, autumn harvest, winter cold. Each season has a great event in a fixed host city: the Great Tournament (Praven), the Great Fair (Shariz), the Harvest Festival (Jelkala) and the Midwinter Feast (Reyvadin).
- **World crises**, one at a time: the Long Winter, the Succession War, the Comet Year, the Iron Tide, the Black Khergit Horde. Foretold ten days ahead; each switchable off.
- **Plague, famine, omens, weather** in battles, storms at sea.
- **Lords are mortal;** heirs inherit. Fallen kingdoms can rise again.
- The rival who hunts you, bounty hunters if you are notorious, and moving fugitives who hunt you back if they escape.
- **Consequences on the map:** ruins left alone are taken by bandits; restored monuments draw pilgrims; the kin of an executed lord ride for you; monasteries raise nearby villages.

## 12. Battles and war

- **Order pages on F4 to F11 (new in 11.8):** F1 to F3 stay the old orders. F4 shows the order card; F5 to F11 open a page over the battle, and a second key gives the order to the listening groups. How to use them, step by step: the [player guide](PLAYER_GUIDE.md), section 11.
- **Formations your men keep:** line in ranks, shield wall, wedge, square, loose order and column. Every man has his place, shields in front. A shield wall halves the damage of arrows and bolts; loose order makes long shots miss; braced spears stop horses.
- **Tactics:** for the foot, advance in order, brace spears, the boar's snout, hold the anvil, skirmish, throw then charge. For the shot, volleys, hold fire till close, the arrow storm, skirmish, a screen in front of the foot, pavises. For the horse, the lance wedge, strike and re-form, the feigned flight, the riding circle, sweeps round either flank, and a reserve.
- **Battle plans:** one order takes the whole army through a battle in stages: Three Battles (Swadian), The Ambush Regiment (Vaegir), The Feigned Flight (Khergit), Shield Wall and Snout (Nord), Hedge and Bolt (Rhodok), Attack and Withdraw (Sarranid), and Hammer and Anvil. Troops fight best in their own people's way.
- **Captains:** enemy and allied lords fight by the plan of their people when they have the troops. Can be switched off (F11, then F10).
- **Your powers:** battle cry, rally, lock shields, loose!, surge and resupply, plus a champion's fury and a last stand. Morale and routing: armies break when most of a side has fallen.
- **Before the fight:** order of battle, stances (entrench, ambush, sneak), a speech, omens, a champion's challenge, skirmishers or prayer. **After it:** prisoners, trophies, spoils, honouring veterans, wounds that need treating.
- **A soldier's life:** enlist with a lord and march with his host: it stays in sight, you keep the map as on any journey (a click and time runs, Space halts, Ctrl+Space hurries), and the H key or the Camp menu opens your place in the ranks, where you can go to your lord or desert at any time. The horns call you into the line when the host fights; the battle is the lord's and his captains give the orders. Six ranks earned by battles stood in the line and by merit, weekly pay on a page of its own, battle money, kit from the quartermaster, leave, discharge, and a record each realm remembers. A sergeant may step forward from your own ranks to serve you.
- **Sieges:** blockades, agents, saboteurs, mines, rams, boiling oil, sallies, parley, poisoned wells, disguises at closed gates, and eighteen camp events.
- **New soldiers:** seven new troop lines, all in vanilla equipment (the Order, the Grey Road, the Guild, pilgrims, settlers, the reborn Ninth Legion, steppe exiles).
- **Free Companies:** contracts, stances, and discipline.

## 13. The tournament festival

- "Join the tournament" opens a **festival of events**, each entered once, with points across the festival.
- Knockout duel of 16 with a drawn bracket; team knockout with a snake draft and your companions; the joust; the fist fight; the archery contest and horse archery; **betting**.
- Prizes, the title of Champion of the Games, recognition, the Black Knight, rivals, the Gauntlet, the squires' lists and a ranking of fighters.

## 14. Money, land and power

- Workshops, caravans, smith commissions, cattle, hunting, a bank, townhouses, rooms, shops and warehouses, a trade ledger, labour for hire, and a weekly budget by category.
- Found a settlement in one of six wild sites; rebuild **Ravenhold**.
- **Fiefs:** tax rates, eleven buildings with real effects, household officers (steward, master-at-arms, chaplain, physician, herald, castellan), revolts if you rule badly, petitioners from the estates.
- **A kingdom of your own:** treasury and taxes, five policy sliders, six laws, estates of the realm, coronation, a royal court, offices and a monthly council, riots, lances, and lords raised from your Free Company captains.
- **Diplomacy:** trade pacts, defensive pacts, alliances, infamy, casus belli, envoys, dictating peace terms.

## 15. Faith, crime and law

- Piety ranks, vows, holy days, pilgrim tokens, relics, monasteries, the schism and the Hierophant's road.
- Crime with Wanted levels per kingdom, trials, bail and chain gangs, pardons, the Wardens' League and its sheriffs.

## 16. Family and legacy

- Marry, raise heirs, take wards, hire tutors. Heirs come of age and take your name. Dedicate victories to a spouse. A happy marriage adds legacy points to spend in your house.

## 17. Rewards

- **62 unique items**, each won by one story: the Ferryman's Hook, Salt-Cured Steel, Jarl's Answer, Wolf-Tooth, the Hooded Cowl, the Khan's Lamellar, the Lantern-Bearer's Mail and many more. Made from Native gear, a notch above the original, never sold, with a line under the item naming the deed.
- **25 captains' prizes** from the road, and twelve masterwork pieces from the master smith's forge.
- **Seven legendary arms** from the great stories: Oathkeeper, Harald Sea-King's Axe, the Bow of the White Falcon, the Blade of the Lantern, Vargan's Sword, the Champion's Surcoat and the Black Hound.
- Named followers, lasting favours from people, titles, rooms, services, and places that change.

## 18. Where to find things

- **Camp > The Chronicle:** the hub for your house, company, court, careers and options.
- **Reports** (map bar): character sheet, Chronicle of Deeds, statistics, the roads, realms, ledger, budget, troop trees.
- **Notes:** the in-game Guide (Game Concepts), realm details (Factions), companions' and lords' notes (Troops), and your stories in progress (Quests).
- **In a battle:** F4 shows the order card. To give an order, choose who listens (1-9, or 0 for all), press the key of a page (F5 formations, F6 foot, F7 shot, F8 horse, F9 battle plans), then the key of the order. The in-game Guide has it under "Reimagined: Battle orders and keys".
- **Options:** switch off omens, raiders, assassins, the rival, crises, tips, hostile wanderers and walk-in places; change difficulty.
- **First visits:** each system explains itself the first time you meet it.
